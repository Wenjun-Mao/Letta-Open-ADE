# Assessment: revise and narrow before building the selector harness

**The evidence justifies investigating how to keep related passages together. It does not yet justify a separate semantic-selection phase as the smallest useful investment.**

My recommendation is to first challenge the four-window restriction with a small offline capacity comparison, and tighten the proposed experiment’s interpretation of a “supported answer.” Then proceed with the bounded selector experiment only if compact selection remains a meaningful requirement.

Three insights would change the next action:

- **The four-window ceiling is doing substantial causal work.** The existing study demonstrates a selection gap under that ceiling, not that the available context budget requires such aggressive selection.
- **A packet can contain the correct answer and still be misleading.** The existing labels deliberately measure answer availability separately from correction visibility. The new investment decision must not collapse those dimensions.
- **Source-only output limits what the selector can fabricate, but not what it can conceal by omission.** Fresh, identical generation/reviewer packets prevent context leakage; they do not provide an independent check on evidence the selector excluded.

The proposed controls, frozen inputs, source-only schema, explicit failure accounting and prohibition on downstream claims are worth retaining. I would not replace this direction with an episode store, correction-keyword system or additional factual reviewer.

## 1. The demonstrated problem is real—but narrower than “we need semantic retrieval”

**Observation.** The correction fixtures contain the missing antecedents. `cases.json` places the original account at E01, several contradictory retellings at E02–E05, and an explicit restoration at E07. In the referential variants, E07 withdraws the mistaken value without naming its replacement. `judgments.json` correctly separates having a named answer from explaining that correction. The readout reports that all selected windows were admitted, with no capacity omissions.   

**Inference.** These fixtures establish that independently ranking exchanges can separate a referring passage from necessary context. They do not establish which repair is preferable: better selection, less restrictive admission, a different retrieval unit, or downstream interpretation of a larger packet.

This distinction matters because `history_ranking.py` describes itself as a finite ranking probe and fixes `TOP_K = 4`. `history_admission.py` separately fixes `MAX_ADMITTED_WINDOWS = 4`; `admit_history()` slices candidates to that limit before capacity checks. `story_packet_builder.build_history_packet()` also asserts that limit. **Four is an explicit count policy, not a number discovered from the token budget.**   

### Smallest next check

Before building a provider-attempt runner, perform an **evaluation-only, whole-pool packet preflight** for an existing failing case, such as D04: retain all eight original exchanges and measure the actual serialized generation and reviewer requests against their existing token limits and full reply reserve.

The readout’s estimates—generation at 877–1,659 against 11,213, reviewer at 8,394–9,204 against 11,469—make this worth checking. They do **not** prove that an eight-window packet fits; that measurement has not been supplied. 

If it fits, the simpler alternative becomes:

> Admit a bounded, eligible whole pool when it fits both consumers’ budgets, rather than first paying another model to reduce it.

That is not automatically the better runtime policy: additional contradictory or irrelevant history might hurt answers. But it is a necessary comparator before claiming that semantic selection is the smallest repair.

**Boundary change:** this diagnostic explicitly revisits ADR 0057’s rejection of extra final slots. It should be recorded as an experimental exception, not silently implemented by changing `TOP_K`, and not treated as approval to expand production history. It leaves PC-03/04/05/06/09/10/11 intact. 

Cost should eventually compare the entire paths:

`selector(pool) + generation(selected) + reviewer(selected)`

versus

`generation(larger packet) + reviewer(larger packet)`.

A smaller final packet alone does not establish a cheaper or faster overall design.

## 2. The proposed neighborhood comparison is legitimate, but unusually narrow

**Observation.** The proposed arm selects one highest-ranked anchor, its immediate predecessor and successor, then fills from the literal top four. It does not expand around multiple relevant passages. 

Applying that recipe to the published baseline orders gives the following **derived predictions—not executed results**:

| Referential case | Predicted neighborhood selection | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| D02, weekday | E02, E01, E03, E04 | Recovers the named original but drops E07’s correction, leaving contradictory weekdays. |
| D04, location | E07, E06, E08, E02 | Keeps the correction, misses E01, and admits unrelated E08. |
| D06, snack | E02, E01, E03, E07 | Includes both the antecedent and correction. |

These follow directly from the frozen source sequence, reported literal order and proposed recipe.   

**Inference.** A semantic victory over this arm would show an advantage over **one-step, single-anchor expansion under four slots**. It would not establish an advantage over retrieval-only context expansion generally. In D04, its weakness is particularly predictable: expanding around E07 cannot reach E01.

I would not respond by testing many neighborhood recipes until one looks competitive. Keep this arm as a clearly named local-context baseline, and use the whole-pool capacity check to challenge the architectural necessity of selection. That adds more decision value than a small search over neighbor heuristics.

### Abstention and source count

N03 is not a symmetric comparison of abstention ability. `rank_windows()` always returns up to four records from a nonempty pool, regardless of relevance; the neighborhood arm also fills slots. Only the semantic arm is instructed to return zero for a no-history request.  

The minimum repair is interpretive, not another mechanism:

**Treat N03 as a semantic-selector conformance control, not evidence that semantic retrieval outperforms simpler retrieval.** Likewise, selecting fewer sources is a cardinality and cost observation, not automatically better dependency handling. There is no need to invent a lexical abstention threshold for this experiment.

The protocol already separates dimensions and prohibits an aggregate quality score. Preserve that discipline in the final investment decision.

## 3. The most important prospective scoring issue is packet-level sufficiency

**Observation.** In `correction_dependencies/assessment.py`, `assess_packet()` computes `named_answer_supported` through set inclusion: any annotated answer route contained in the admitted set earns the field. Adding contradictory sources does not remove that credit. The frozen judgments intentionally allow E01 alone as a named-answer route.  

That is appropriate for the historical question **“is a named answer available?”** It is not sufficient for **“does this packet justify a confident answer?”**

### Smallest counterexample: use existing D02, without new text

Consider an admitted packet containing **E01 and E02**, but not E07:

E01 says Monday. E02 says Wednesday. The packet contains the author-labeled correct value, but lacks the passage explaining why the later account should be rejected.

Under the old measurements, this can correctly register:

- a named answer is present;
- a mistaken retelling is visible;
- the correction is absent.

It must not thereby become a prospectively successful packet for confidently answering “Monday.” Neither source chronology nor the evaluator’s knowledge of omitted E07 can settle the contradiction for the final consumers. This follows from the actual D02 text and assessment logic.  

**Recommendation.** Use that packet as an offline check of the new decision rubric. Keep the historical fields and artifacts unchanged, but require the proposed **qualification/ambiguity assessment** to block an investment claim based merely on answer presence in a materially misleading packet.

This is not a recommendation to require every correction in every answer. An original-only packet and a packet containing unresolved competing claims are different cases. Nor does it require a new composite metric: the protocol already proposes necessary qualifications and unresolved meanings. Make their effect on the pass/fail decision explicit. 

### Source IDs preserve provenance, not neutrality

A selector choosing among authentic sources is still making an interpretive choice. It can implicitly select a “true story” by excluding the material that makes another account plausible.

Your planned N01 is the right place to expose this. Its deletion challenge should include a structurally valid packet containing one plausible antecedent and the referring passage, while omitting the second plausible antecedent. The source validator should accept its identities; the semantic assessment should reject its false clarity.

N02 should test the complementary direction: **a seemingly complete answer must still be rejected when a later supplied correction makes it stale**. Dependencies are not only missing antecedents of selected references; they also include qualifications that undermine an otherwise self-contained source.

These are refinements of the planned controls, not reasons to add more cases or expand the selector’s output beyond source IDs.

## 4. Preserve meaning through the final wire format—not just through receipts

**Observation.** The proposed selector sees original message IDs and within-chat sequences. In contrast, `natural_memory_binding.build_natural_binding_map()` serializes history messages with request-local H handles, roles, content, hashes and timestamps, but **not their original sequence numbers**. Conversation identity remains present. The readout explicitly acknowledges that original identities and conversation-wide sequences are retained in receipts rather than exposed in the H wire format.   

The current fixtures use distinct, orderly timestamps, so this is **not an observed failure in the six cases**. But final H order follows selection/admission priority, not necessarily chronology. A future dependency that requires sequence information could be understandable to the selector and evaluator yet ambiguous to both final consumers.

The minimum requirement is:

> Judge semantic availability from the actual admitted generation/reviewer payload, not from richer source-order receipts.

If a new control requires sequence information that disappears during serialization, either preserve that ordinary provenance field in H or mark the control unsupported by the existing wire contract. Do not count source-ID membership alone as successful delivery of the needed meaning. No selector rationale or rewritten history is necessary.

### Leakage versus fixture regularity

The protocol’s explicit exclusion of `source_topology`, labels, expected IDs and answer values is good. The inspected `context.json` really does contain evaluator commentary identifying the restoration relationship, so wholesale serialization would be consequential leakage.  

A separate risk remains: all six existing cases put the original at E01 and the correction at E07, using closely matched restoration language. That is fixture regularity, not proof of improper leakage.

Keep those frozen cases unchanged. In the **already planned new controls**, avoid reusing that decisive-source positioning and restoration pattern. N02 is especially useful if its correct route is not “recover the first account.” Do not randomly scramble chronology when chronology is meaningful evidence, and do not call three renamed episode dimensions independent replication.

## 5. A positive result can still be irrelevant to continuity

Two limits matter most for the next investment decision.

### The relevant source may never reach the selector

`persistence/history.py::read_history_corpus()` orders completed exchanges by assistant timestamp descending and takes at most 128 before content and annotation exclusions. It checks workspace, subject, purpose and character root, and does not exclude archived conversations. This is source-code inspection, not a verified database execution. 

Consequently, “present somewhere in ADE history” and “available in the selector pool” are different conditions. The proposed eight-record experiment intentionally assumes the latter. Its success cannot establish recall across sufficiently long relationships.

M01 usefully tests a missing antecedent while retaining its correction. A more dangerous logical counterpart is **an available old answer with an unavailable correction**. Two underlying histories could then present an identical visible pool but require different current answers. No source-ID selector can distinguish those worlds.

I would **not** build a larger-history retrieval benchmark now. Retain candidate availability as a separate prerequisite, and do not let a positive selection result authorize runtime integration before that prerequisite is investigated.

### Better evidence may not change the answer

The proposed campaign dispatches no generator or reviewer, and `story_packet_builder.py` uses an explicit synthetic reviewer reply. It therefore cannot show whether repaired packets improve recall, preserve uncertainty naturally or prevent invented participation. This limitation is correctly declared, not a hidden defect.  

**My judgment:** a favorable selection result should lead to a small behavioral falsification test, not another round of selector sophistication.

The smallest useful later witness is one fresh answer per condition on a representative case: the weaker packet versus the repaired packet, with identical current context and no selector reasoning. Where the whole pool fits, the larger-packet alternative may be the more decision-relevant comparator. Ask whether the repaired evidence actually changes a wrong or inadequately qualified response into a supported one.

That would still be a diagnostic, not a reliability estimate or reviewer/persistence qualification.

**Boundary change:** dispatching those answers would require separate authorization and would depart from this protocol’s zero-generation-call boundary. I recommend neither doing it now nor silently folding it into the ten selector attempts.

## Minimum revised path

I would make only these changes before implementing the model runner:

1. **Resolve the capacity question offline.** Test whether a whole-pool packet fits existing generation/reviewer token budgets, explicitly documenting the evaluation-only departure from four windows.
2. **Make the decision rubric reject misleading packets.** Use existing D02’s contradictory subset as the smallest check; exercise ambiguity loss in N01 and stale-original selection in N02. Preserve all historical labels and results.
3. **Keep the proposed comparison modest.** Label neighborhood expansion narrowly, treat N03 as conformance rather than comparative abstention evidence, and assess meaning from final wire payloads.

Then there are two reasonable outcomes:

**If the whole pool fits and compactness has no independently important benefit, defer the semantic runner.** The next useful question is whether simpler admission improves actual answers without harmful distraction.

**If compact selection remains important, proceed with the revised ten-case campaign as a feasibility/falsification exercise.** Retain the one-attempt policy, existing route subject to later verification, no repairs or rerolls, source-only output and fresh shared packets. No model sweep, new store or generic selector framework is justified.

The distinction is important: **“worth testing under a deliberate compactness constraint” is defensible; “shown to be the smallest continuity repair” is not yet defensible.**

## What remains undecidable

This evidence cannot decide whether larger packets harm conversational quality, whether semantic selection’s gains survive less templated language, whether actual candidate retrieval supplies the needed corrections, or whether the added request improves end-to-end quality enough to justify its latency and cost. Nor does it verify archive/version behavior against a database, actual provider behavior, reviewer interpretation or persistence correctness.

Those uncertainties do not invalidate a bounded experiment. They determine what a positive result is allowed to purchase next.
