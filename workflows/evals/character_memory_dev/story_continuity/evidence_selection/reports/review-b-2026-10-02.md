# Assessment: REVISE before building the selector runner

**Semantic selection is a plausible hypothesis, but it is not yet the smallest justified next investment.** I would retain the direction, revise the experiment, and proceed first with limited offline preparation—not runtime integration or a substantial provider-campaign harness.

Three findings would change my next action:

1. **The proposed neighborhood arm exposes a distinction between finding the correct value and supplying a packet that actually supports choosing it.** That distinction needs to govern the decision rule.
2. **The evidence does not establish a need to compress these eight short exchanges into four.** Check that constraint before paying for semantic compression.
3. **A selector victory confined to the existing fixture structure would be weak evidence for further investment.** Use the already-planned new controls to break that structure, without expanding the benchmark.

The proposal has important strengths: original-source-only output, fresh downstream requests, explicit invalid-output handling, separate availability and admission accounting, and no automatic adoption claim. I would preserve those boundaries. The main repair is to make the experiment more decision-informative, not to add another evaluator or memory architecture.

## 1. The smallest useful counterexample is already implied by the proposed comparison

### Observation: the existing score measures value availability, not whole-packet sufficiency

In `correction_dependencies/assessment.py`, `assess_packet()` defines `named_answer_supported` through `supported("answer_routes")`: it is true when **any labeled answer route is a subset of the admitted sources**. It separately reports correction presence, dependency status and mistaken retellings. That is appropriate for the historical availability measurement, but it does not establish that the complete packet warrants a settled answer. 

Applying the proposed anchor-neighborhood recipe to the recorded baseline orders gives the following. **These are deductions from the specified recipe and recorded orders, not newly executed results.**

| Referential case | Derived neighborhood selection | What the packet contains |
|---|---|---|
| D02: weekday | `E02, E01, E03, E04` | The original Monday account, three Wednesday accounts, **no correction** |
| D04: location | `E07, E06, E08, E02` | The referential correction, but **no named antecedent**; also unrelated E08 |
| D06: snack | `E02, E01, E03, E07` | The original, mistaken retellings and the correction restoring the original |

The derivation follows directly from `PROTOCOL.md`, “Three Frozen Arms,” the baseline orders in `READOUT.md`, and the exchange sequence in `cases.json`.   

**D02 is consequential.** Under the frozen availability labels, the neighborhood packet has a named-answer route because E01 is present. But a fresh reader sees Monday versus three Wednesday accounts without E07 explaining which accounts were mistaken. The packet itself supports uncertainty; it does not establish that Monday is the settled answer.

This is not a defect in the historical report, which explicitly disclaims answer-quality measurement. It is a trap for the proposed decision rule: “neighborhood expansion matches the useful results” must not mean merely matching named-answer availability.  

### Minimum revision

Keep the frozen labels and their historical interpretation unchanged. For the new comparison, make the decision rule explicitly consider **the whole admitted packet**, including whether it retains the material correction or qualification needed to interpret competing accounts.

In particular:

- `named_answer_supported = true` is not, by itself, a settled-answer success.
- A packet containing E01 plus conflicting retellings without their correction must not be treated as equivalent to E01+E07.
- E01 alone can remain a valid bare-answer route under the existing labels. Do not retroactively require every correction explanation for every question.

This needs no new model judge or aggregate score. A prospective, source-quoted packet assessment can use the dimensions already proposed.

**The smallest useful test is D02’s derived neighborhood packet versus the existing E01+E07 reference.** It requires no additional fixture and no provider call to expose the difference between answer-value availability and interpretable evidence.

## 2. The four-window limit is currently a reason to investigate—not a demonstrated requirement

### Observation

The four-window restriction exists in multiple places:

- `history_ranking.py`: `TOP_K = 4`, used by `rank_windows()`.
- `history_admission.py`: `MAX_ADMITTED_WINDOWS = 4`, with `admit_history()` slicing the candidate list.
- `story_packet_builder.py`: `build_history_packet()` asserts that the selected count does not exceed that limit.   

The completed readout reports generation estimates of **877–1,659 against 11,213**, and reviewer estimates, including the full reply reserve, of **8,394–9,204 against 11,469**. Those results establish that the tested packets fit. They do **not** establish that all eight exchanges would fail to fit, or that four is the appropriate compression target. 

### Inference

The evidence supports:

> Independent-window selection under a four-window constraint can omit a necessary antecedent.

It does not yet support:

> An additional semantic model phase is preferable to retaining more of this already-small candidate pool.

A selector might solve a restriction imposed by the probe rather than a restriction imposed by the product.

### Minimum revision

Before implementing the provider runner, add an **offline full-pool capacity diagnostic**: determine whether both downstream requests can carry all eight original exchanges under the same token budgets and reply reserve.

This is not a fourth tuned retrieval algorithm. It is a sensitivity check on the premise of compression.

Do not simply pass eight IDs into the current builder and call the result an eight-source test: its assertion and the admission slice prevent that. The diagnostic must explicitly account for those restrictions.  

**Boundary change:** this recommendation revisits ADR 0057’s rejection of extra final slots and the protocol’s four-window envelope **for an offline diagnostic only**. It does not recommend changing the runtime default. That amendment should be explicit. 

If all eight fit, that does not prove that supplying all eight produces better answers: extra conflicting material could also make interpretation harder. But it changes the next question from “Can a model find the missing source?” to:

> Is semantic compression beneficial compared with preserving the available context within the existing budget?

If four remains fixed, the experiment remains legitimate—but its conclusion must remain conditional on that imposed constraint.

## 3. The comparison is usable, but a positive result needs to survive the fixture shortcut

### Observation: six cases largely instantiate one structure

Across the three families, E01 supplies the restored value, E02–E05 repeat a mistaken value, E06 adds a compatible detail, E07 restores the original, and E08 is unrelated. The referential corrections all direct attention back to the initial account. 

Consequently, a strategy resembling “take the original and the restoration” can succeed without demonstrating broader ability to identify evidence dependencies. Removing answer keys and `source_topology` from the request is necessary, but it cannot remove this structural shortcut.

The neighborhood derivation also shows why a semantic victory only on the location family would be limited evidence: it would beat **this particular highest-anchor, immediate-neighbor recipe** where its anchor sits far from the antecedent. It would not establish superiority to retrieval-only approaches generally.

### Minimum revision: use N01 and N02 to carry the new information

Do not add more noun substitutions or tune neighborhood sizes against these known outcomes. Instead, construct the already-planned new controls so that at least one meaningful comparison is not another E01/E07 restoration problem.

For example, N02 can have an irrelevant opening, an original account later in the conversation, a genuine correction at a non-terminal position, and subsequent material that does not establish a new truth. N01 can use genuinely competing, nonadjacent antecedents rather than another instruction to recover “the first account.”

This changes neither the ten-case ceiling nor the source-ID-only design. Preserve actual chronology; do not shuffle meaningful conversation order merely to obscure positions.

I would also strengthen the continuation criterion:

> Further selector investment should require a useful advantage over both simpler arms on at least one newly authored, structurally different case—not solely a gain on the exposed restoration fixtures.

That is a stricter experimental decision rule, not a statistical generalization claim. The six existing cases remain useful regression checks.

### Abstention and source count: distinguish an asymmetric policy comparison from an unfair one

The deterministic arms fill four slots whenever enough candidates exist. The semantic arm may return fewer or none. Therefore N03’s empty-selection advantage is substantially determined by the policies being compared; it is not a matched test of abstention capability. 

Nevertheless, fewer unnecessary admissions can be a real policy advantage. I would **not** force the semantic arm to fill four slots, nor add an unvalidated lexical threshold merely to make the arms look symmetrical.

Keep N03 as a guard against unnecessary retrieval. Report source count and irrelevant admissions separately, and describe the asymmetry. The existing requirement for an available-answer or dependency gain already prevents an empty N03 result alone from justifying continuation; preserve that requirement. 

Likewise, one frozen model configuration and one attempt per case are reasonable for this bounded diagnostic. They cannot establish repeated-run reliability, select a production model, or justify high-thinking selection on every conversational turn.

## 4. Source IDs constrain fabrication, but selection is still a semantic decision

**Source-ID-only output is worth preserving.** It prevents the selector from substituting its own rewritten history for the original evidence. It does not make the selector neutral: choosing which sources to omit can determine which account downstream models perceive as credible.

A hypothetical example illustrates the limit. Suppose a selected source says “Monday,” while an omitted genuine correction says “Wednesday.” The selected source can pass every identity, scope and hash check. Fresh generator and reviewer requests can contain exactly the same valid packet. Neither request can recover the omitted correction from that packet.

Thus:

> Shared evidence removes an evidence mismatch between generator and reviewer. It does not make either an independent check on the selector’s omissions.

This is consistent with PC-05’s distinction between source validity and interpretation. It is not an argument for a second factual reviewer. 

### What the assessment must protect

Dependencies are not only “this admitted reference needs that antecedent.” They also include:

> This selected assertion becomes misleading unless an otherwise unselected qualification is retained.

N02 should test that relationship. N01 should require the evidence supporting the competing interpretations, rather than rewarding either a silently chosen account or a vacuous empty packet.

There need not be one unique correct ID list. The desired property is that the selected packet preserves what matters for this question—including a reason to remain uncertain when appropriate.

### A concrete packet-format caveat

In `natural_memory_binding.py`, `build_natural_binding_map()` puts message handles, roles, content, hashes and timestamps into the history wire format. Conversation/version identity is retained at the exchange level. **Original within-chat sequence numbers are not included in the message wire records.** The readout explicitly notes that sequence numbers exist in `source_order` receipts rather than the H wire format.  

That distinction matters because the selector is proposed to receive sequence information. An evaluator must not use that richer input or an external receipt to declare that the fresh answering request has enough information.

**Hypothesis, not an observed fixture failure:** an ordinal reference, omitted intervening exchange, or timestamp tie could make sequence information necessary, particularly because selected sources are presented in admission-priority order.

The minimum action is to inspect sufficiency on the **actual final H payload**. Do not change the wire format speculatively. If a concrete control demonstrates the need, forwarding original structural sequence metadata would be a small, explicit wire-contract change—not forwarding the selector’s reasoning.

## 5. What a positive result would—and would not—justify

A favorable revised result could justify this narrow statement:

> With necessary evidence already available in a small pool, this fixed selector produced more useful final evidence packets than these two fixed alternatives on these development cases.

That would be useful. It would still leave three major questions unresolved.

**Candidate availability.** The inspected `read_history_corpus()` selects the newest scoped completed pairs, with `MAX_EXCHANGES = 128`, then applies content and annotation eligibility checks to the retained rows. An older original can therefore be absent while a later correction remains available. The eight-exchange experiment cannot establish that production candidate construction will recover the necessary material. 

M01 is a useful missing-antecedent check, but it is comparatively transparent: the retained correction itself signals that something was wrong. Missing a correction while retaining an apparently complete old account is a different, less visible absence problem. A positive M01 result must not become a general safe-abstention claim. I would record this limit, not add another case now.

**Downstream benefit.** Constructing fresh requests verifies an important boundary; it cannot show that a generator uses the improved evidence, that the reviewer catches misuse, or that uncertainty sounds natural. `build_history_packet()` deliberately uses a synthetic reviewer-reply placeholder rather than an observed answer. 

After a favorable selection pilot, the smallest useful next experiment would be a **paired evidence-restoration test**: keep the downstream setup and four-source count fixed, retain the correction, and replace one nonessential selected exchange with the missing antecedent. Use fresh requests and inspect the resulting answers.

That tests whether fixing the measured availability gap changes the behavior you care about. It does not require first deploying the semantic selector.

**Boundary change:** dispatching those generation/reviewer requests is outside the current protocol and would require a separately approved experiment. I am not recommending adding them silently to this campaign.

**Operational value.** The proposed route, high-thinking setting, output reserve and deadline define an experimental envelope, not an established interactive-service choice. Actual latency, usage, failure behavior and benefit remain unmeasured here. Keep the existing observational accounting; do not turn this review into a model bake-off or new spending-control system. 

## Recommended next action

**Approve only a revised, small offline preparation slice**, with three concrete changes:

| Change | Purpose | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Check full-pool downstream capacity | Determine whether the four-slot constraint is creating the immediate need for compression | Explicit experimental-envelope amendment; no runtime change |
| Make whole-packet qualifications govern the comparison | Prevent named-value presence from masquerading as a settled, interpretable answer | Preserve historical labels; clarify prospective assessment and decision rules |
| Make N01/N02 structurally different and require a useful new-case gain | Reduce the chance that success merely exploits E01/E07 restoration structure | Same ten-case ceiling; stronger continuation criterion |

Keep the existing source-integrity validation, strict output schema, fresh-packet isolation and one-attempt semantics. Do not add an episode store, dependency graph, selector-written summaries, semantic keyword rules, second reviewer or generic framework.

If the offline work shows that a simpler context-preservation approach supplies equally useful evidence, stop before building the selector runner. If a meaningful joint-evidence gap remains and the revised controls can distinguish it, the bounded selector experiment becomes a proportionate next investigation.

This preserves the relevant product intent—particularly PC-03/04/10/11 continuity and ownership, PC-05 structural-versus-semantic responsibility, and PC-06/09’s exclusions. The proposed changes concern the investigation, not permission to rewrite character history.
